# The Devils in the Details

Design notes for a Fallout 4 mod set on Fort Bragg, NC, in the 1-504 PIR barracks area as it was in the early 1990s.
Started 2026-10-22. Nothing here is final.

## Story

### The premise

Two hundred years after the bombs, the soldiers at Fort Bragg are still there. The garrison's last
order was to **hold the post until relieved**. Nobody came. The descendants kept the order, along with
drill, rank and the regulations, which by now are close to a religion.

They protect the Sandhills from the **raiders and super mutants who attack regularly**. In return
they take a **tithe**: food, water and recruits. Whether that's protection or a racket depends on who
you ask.

**The question the mod asks:** the settlers hate the soldiers, but would they survive without them?

### The soldiers ("the Devils")

- Descendants of the pre-war garrison. Locals call them the **Devils**, after the 504th's WWII
  nickname, "Devils in Baggy Pants", half in fear and half in respect.
- Disciplined, well armed, short of people. Every year they need more food and more recruits
  to hold the line.
- They honestly believe they're the only thing between the settlers and death. They may be
  right.
- They need: someone to show both a decent officer and a hard one. A **reform-minded officer**
  who thinks the tithe has become extortion; a **hardliner** who thinks the settlers owe them
  everything.

### RonMike, the perfect soldier

**Iron Mike** ("The Airborne Trooper") is the 15-foot statue of a paratrooper unveiled on Bragg in
1961. In the early 90s it stood in the traffic circle between the Fort Bragg Club and the XVIII
Airborne Corps headquarters. It's in the mod for atmosphere, not for a quest.

- **The letters wore off.** Two hundred years of weather took the "I" from the plaque, and what's
  left reads **RON MIKE**. The Devils worship him as **RonMike, the perfect soldier**: the one who
  never broke, never quit and never left his post. The regulations are his scripture.
- **The faith in small things:** recruits are sworn in at his feet; soldiers touch his boot for
  luck before a patrol; the dead are named to him; "RonMike sees you" is both a blessing and a
  warning. Fragments of the worn plaque are recited as a creed, with the gaps filled in by
  guesswork.
- **The hardliner and the reformer** read him differently: one as the soldier who obeys, the
  other as the soldier who protects.
- **Top knows better.** The pre-war ghouls remember a statue. "Ron Mike? It's *Iron* Mike, son."
  Whether the player tells the Devils is up to them.
- **Where he stands (decided):** in front of the **82nd Airborne Division headquarters**, in the
  southwest corner of the barracks map. The real traffic circle isn't on the map, so the Devils
  moved him there, all 3,235 pounds, as the centerpiece of their own headquarters. (He was moved
  for real in 1979, so there's precedent.)
- **The plaque text** will be written for the mod; the real inscription doesn't need to be copied.

### Devils slang and fool's errands

Two hundred years on, the Devils still talk like the 82nd, even when nobody remembers what the
words meant. Use these in dialogue and background barks.

- **Sayings:**
  - "Ate up like a soup sandwich." (Nobody's seen a sandwich of any kind in generations.)
  - "You look like a monkey trying to fuck a football." (A drill sergeant's line for a recruit
    fumbling with gear. Fallout 4 is rated M and its raiders swear constantly, so it fits.)
  - "Beat your face, private!" and "Hold down my ground." Both mean: drop and do push-ups.
- **The greeting:** an enlisted soldier passing an officer salutes and says **"All the way, sir!"**
  (the 82nd's motto). The officer returns it with "Airborne!"
  - **A hook for Nate:** once the Devils know he's a commissioned officer, enlisted Devils salute
    him with "All the way, sir!" as he passes: a small, constant reminder of what his credentials
    mean to them. The hardliner's loyal soldiers might pointedly not salute. If the Devils turn
    hostile, the salutes stop.
- **Fool's errands** the old hands send new recruits on, straight out of the motor pool:
  - Go to another shop for a **Yadkin extension**. (Yadkin is the road; there's no such part.)
  - Fetch a **bottle of blinker fluid**.
  - Let the **winter air out of the tires** and fill them with **summer air**.
  - Could be a small, funny side quest: a Devils sergeant sends the player on one. The settlers at
    the Cue and Ale market have been in on the joke for two hundred years and sell "blinker fluid"
    to every recruit who comes looking.
  - Other classics to consider: a box of grid squares, a can of prop wash, the keys to the drop
    zone. (Careful with that last one: it might be how the Key to the Post started.)
- Add more as they come up.

### The settlers

- Survivors from Fayetteville. **Decided: they live on Bragg Boulevard** (see "Bragg Boulevard"
  below), not on farms: survivors gravitate toward what's left of a city, and farmland would be
  boring to build and to walk. Any fields they work are off the map.
- They resent the tithe, which keeps growing; conscription, which takes their children; and the
  curfew and checkpoints.
- They're also raided constantly, and the soldiers are what stops the worst of it. Some know it.
- They need: a **moderate leader** who wants a fairer deal, and a **hard core** who wants the
  soldiers gone whatever the cost.

### Bragg Boulevard: the settlers' home

The strip just outside post is the Fayetteville every soldier from the early 90s remembers: pawn
shops, tattoo parlors, used-car lots, bars, strip malls. It's a **third small worldspace**,
condensed the way Fallout 4 condenses Boston: one stretch of the boulevard, with downtown
Fayetteville as a skyline past the playable edge.

- **The heart of town: the Cue and Ale strip mall.** The Cue and Ale was a strip club in a
  U-shaped strip mall with a huge parking lot: **Sycamore Square Shopping Center**, which also
  had a Food Lion. Today's listings give 3308-3450 Bragg Blvd, around 35.0780 N, 78.9348 W. **It has
  to be in the mod.** The settlers' town grows out of it:
  - **The Cue and Ale itself** is the settlers' bar and gathering place, its sign still up two
    hundred years on. It's where the player meets people, hears rumors and picks up work.
  - **The parking lot is the market:** booths and stalls inside the U, where the settlers sell crops
    from the fields and things they make: light armor, clothes, homemade goods. The settlers aren't
    just people the Devils tax; they're the region's market.
  - **The other storefronts** get whatever names and uses the town needs (a clothier, an armor
    maker, the settler leaders' office). Any we don't want to build collapse into rubble.
  - **What it looks like** (from a recent street photo, kept locally in `data/reference/`, not in
    the repo since it isn't ours): a long, one-story brown brick building across the back of the
    lot, with a **covered walkway of round brick arches** along the storefronts; brick wings on the
    sides; a huge flat lot in front; pines behind; and **two tall lattice radio masts** rising
    behind it. The arches make a good modular kit piece, and the masts are a landmark you can see
    from the freeway and the checkpoint (and maybe where a signal comes from).
  - Still to do: check the mall's 1990s footprint in an aerial photo of the area. The photo is
    from today, so the details may have changed since the 90s.
- **A handful of enterable interiors**, the rest sealed, as on post. The settler leaders live and
  work here, and the codebook is kept here.
- **The Devils' checkpoint.** Bragg was an open post in the 90s, with no gates. The Devils built a
  makeshift one: wrecked cars, sandbags, a guard shack across the road into town. That's where
  the tithe, the curfew and the checkpoints from the settlers' complaints become something the
  player walks through. **Decided: on Yadkin Road.** The All-American Freeway runs parallel to
  Bragg Blvd and never meets it; Yadkin meets Bragg Blvd at a Y intersection (where the McDonald's
  was). The checkpoint sits at the map edge on Yadkin, and the stretch of Yadkin between post and
  town is condensed out.
- **The area (decided):** a four-sided outline taking in the end of Yadkin (the way in), a stretch
  of the All-American Freeway (a freeway, like Boston's), and part of Bragg Blvd:
  - 35°04'56.60"N 78°58'10.73"W
  - 35°04'11.10"N 78°56'05.25"W
  - 35°04'46.18"N 78°55'46.34"W
  - 35°05'42.02"N 78°57'10.86"W

  The southeast edge was moved out 400 m from the first draft, which put Sycamore Square right on
  the edge. About 3.5 x 2.1 km at its longest, **4.9 km² (about 1,420 cells)**, a little bigger
  than the barracks map. The worldspace grid runs north-south, so the box around it is 63 x 48
  cells; the
  corners outside the outline are non-playable margin. (The first outline, 12.7 km² along Bragg
  Blvd, was nearly four times the barracks map and was dropped.)
- **Detail where it counts:** the strip around the Y gets the work. The freeway is mostly road
  pieces and wrecks, and the **wooded areas become the settlers' fields**: visible from the
  street and the freeway, but not built for exploring.
- **Two ways in:** the road through the Devils' checkpoint (a map-edge transition to the barracks
  area), and a storm drain branch of the underground that comes up in town. The drains are how the
  settlers smuggle around the tithe, and how the raiders get in.
- **The vents are on post,** so venting the hot zone never touches the town.

### Raiders and super mutants

Not a side to choose: a constant pressure. Regular attacks on the farms and the post are what make
the soldiers necessary, and what make the player's choice cost something. Whatever the ending,
the raids continue: whoever is left has to deal with them.

### The player's hook

**Nate is a pre-war Army veteran: the only living person with valid 2077 military credentials.**

- The **armory**, deep in the underground complex, still recognizes an active-duty officer's ID.
  Suddenly both sides need the player.
- **But the ID is only half of it** (see "The MacGuffin" below): the armory works on the
  two-person rule, and the other half is held by the factions themselves.
- Unlocking it for the soldiers makes them unstoppable. Giving it to the settlers flips the
  balance. Or the player can destroy it.
- **Why go to North Carolina:** a 2077 emergency broadcast, picked up in Boston (see "The
  recall" below).

### The MacGuffin: the Key to the Post and the codebook

Real weapons storage used the **two-person rule**: nobody opens it alone. The armory needs
**a valid officer's ID (Nate) plus a 2077 authenticator in two parts**, and each faction holds one
part without knowing what it is.

- **The Key to the Post (the Devils have it).** For two hundred years, every commander of the
  Devils has worn a battered metal key on a chain as the badge of office, handed over at every
  change of command. It's sacred to them, and nobody remembers what it opens.
- **The codebook (the settlers have it).** A sealed authentication codebook: the day's code words,
  like the real-world "cookie" authenticators. A scavenger pulled it out of a pre-war office
  generations ago; the settlers keep it as a curiosity, or close to a holy text. Perhaps the
  moderate settler leader is its keeper.
- **How the player gets both is the choice:**
  - **With the Devils:** they hand over the key; the codebook has to be traded for, stolen or
    taken by force from the settlers.
  - **With the settlers:** the player takes the key from the Devils' commander. To the Devils
    that's about the worst insult there is.
  - **Peace:** both leaders bring their halves to the armory door together. The final scene.
- **The way in is its own problem.** Even with the ID, the key and the codebook, nobody can reach
  the armory door: it sits in a radiation hot zone swarming with feral ghouls. Getting there means
  restarting the reactor (see "The hot zone and the reactor" below), a separate quest that can be
  done before or after the player picks a side.

### The underground

Not realistic, and doesn't need to be: this is Fallout, and every pre-war base had a secret.

- **A tunnel complex under the post**, with an entrance in each of the two exterior areas: one near
  the drop zone (found early, since the player lands near it) and one in the barracks area (under
  the headquarters building or a motor pool, perhaps).
- **Its pre-war cover story:** officially a utility tunnel, an emergency command bunker or
  munitions storage. Terminals and holotapes reveal what it really was.
- **It explains the raids:** the raiders and mutants get past the Devils' patrols through the
  tunnels. Sealing them, or leaving them open, can be part of the ending.
- **Who's down there:** feral ghouls who were the pre-war garrison, and a few who never went
  feral (see below); automated defenses still following 2077 orders; settlers using the storm
  drain branch to Bragg Boulevard to smuggle goods around the tithe.
- **Shape: three ways from the drop zone entrance.** Past Vargas's patrol, the tunnels split:
  - **East: the raiders.** A raider camp, with their own version of a guard gate across the
    tunnel. This is the raiders' road onto the post.
  - **West: the ghouls.** Top's squad, and past them the heavily irradiated hot zone and the
    armory at its heart. The player can meet Top early, before ever reaching Bragg.
  - **North: Bragg.** The way out into the barracks area, and the quest marker for division
    headquarters.
  - **Still to place:** the reactor, and the storm drain branch to Bragg Boulevard. Idea: both lie
    **east, past the raider gate.** Top already says the way to the reactor runs through
    raider-held tunnels, and a raider-held drain into town is how the raiders reach the settlers.
  - All of it: utility tunnels, pipes, steam, flooded sections.

### The hot zone and the reactor

**The armory sits behind a wall of radiation, and the radiation is full of feral ghouls.**

- **Why it's hot:** when the bombs fell, the reactor scrammed and the exhaust fans stopped. Whatever
  leaked in the deep levels in 2077 has hung in the dead air ever since. (Could the source be in the
  armory itself? That would hint at what's inside.)
- **The ferals** are what's left of the pre-war garrison: the soldiers who stayed at their posts
  as the air went bad. They still crowd the corridors around the armory door, which is why nobody
  has looted it in two hundred years.
- **The ghouls who didn't turn.** Living at the edge of the hot zone, among and behind the ferals,
  is a small squad of non-feral ghouls: **the original 2077 garrison**, the soldiers the Devils are
  descended from, or their comrades. They got the same order: hold the post until relieved.
  - **Their leader** is a pre-war NCO (a first sergeant, "Top", fits: the one who kept the unit
    together). He tells the player plainly: **nobody gets to that armory until the power's back on.
    The fans have to run to vent the air.**
  - **Why they can't do it themselves:** they're too few, the way to the reactor runs through
    raider-held tunnels, and the reactor controls want an officer's authorization (Nate again).
    Perhaps also: Top believes the hot air is what turned his people feral, and every year he stays
    by the armory he loses another one.
  - **How they treat the ferals:** as their own soldiers. Top doesn't want them killed for sport,
    but once the air clears, "put them down clean" is the last order he gives about them, and his
    squad fights beside the player.
  - **What they think of the Devils and the settlers:** a third view on the mod's question. They
    remember the post before the Devils became what they are. They may never have gone up to see.
- **Restarting the reactor** is a dungeon of its own: down the reactor branch, past raiders and
  dormant defenses, to bring the reactor online. Doing so:
  1. Starts the exhaust fans. The hot zone clears over a short timed sequence (sirens, fans spinning
     up, the green haze thinning).
  2. Wakes the automated defenses, which still follow 2077 orders and don't know who anyone is.
  3. Restores light and power through the complex (doors, elevators, terminals).
  4. **Powers the post.** The reactor was the post's power plant, so the lights come back on in the
     barracks area too. The Devils gain from it whoever turns it on. (Real precedent: the Army ran
     small reactors at posts and remote sites; SM-1 at Fort Belvoir fed the post's grid from 1957.)
- **The choice at the reactor: vent now, or fix the filters first.** **Decided:** the exhaust vents
  come out **on post, among the Devils' barracks**, disguised as something ordinary (a boiler plant
  stack or a building's ventilation). The filters that should scrub the air failed long ago. At the
  reactor the player decides:
  - **Turn it on now.** The hot zone clears at once, and the poisoned air pours out over the
    barracks. A lot of Devils die, and the Devils turn hostile for good. A player siding with the
    settlers might do it on purpose, as a weapon: the darkest way to win.
  - **Fix the filters first.** Find the materials to repair them (a scavenging run through the
    tunnels, or a trade with one of the factions), then start the reactor. Slower, and nobody dies.
- **Ending Top's story: Nate relieves the post.** Once the way is clear, Nate, the only officer
  with valid credentials, formally relieves Top and his squad. Two hundred years after the order,
  someone finally came. This ends the hot zone quest and opens the way to the armory.

**Technical notes:**
- **The player always has power armor** (it's required for the jump), and power armor resists
  radiation. Rads alone won't stop them. The block needs to be a hard one: a **radiation interlock**
  on the blast door into the hot zone that won't open until the air reading is safe, with the
  radiation high enough that a player who gets past it some other way still can't survive.
- Radiation as hazard trigger volumes in the hot zone, disabled by a script on the quest stage that
  starts the fans. Fans as animated activators, switched on by the same stage.
- The ferals stay after the air clears; only the radiation goes.

### Story shape

1. **Arrival.** The jump; the raid on the drop zone. Both sides are neutral. Small jobs for each;
   learn how they see things. Witness a raid, and see what the soldiers do (and what they charge
   for it).
2. **Rising tension.** Something forces the issue: a tithe raid on a farm, an ambush on a patrol,
   a missing recruit. The player learns what the armory is, and that it needs more than an ID.
3. **The choice.** Help one side; **the other becomes hostile**. Get the key and the codebook.
4. **The ending.** The armory decides who wins, or how it ends.
5. **Optional third path.** Speech checks bring the reform officer and the moderate settler leader
   together for a negotiated peace: a fairer tithe, volunteers instead of conscription, and both
   halves of the authenticator brought to the door together.

### Consequences to show

- **Side with the soldiers:** the settlers are safe from raids and live under the Devils for good.
  Hardliner or reformer in charge changes how that feels.
- **Side with the settlers:** they're free, and the raids get worse. Can they hold? Maybe the
  player has to help them build defenses, or maybe some farms are lost.
- **Negotiated peace:** the raids continue, but now the two sides face them together.

### The recall: how it starts

**The broadcast.** Wandering toward the south end of Boston, Nate picks up a radio signal, a
2077 recording still on its loop:

> "This is not a drill. The United States has been hit by nuclear weapons. Everyone is ordered to
> stand their post until relieved or until further orders arrive. All officers are required to
> report to division headquarters to get assignments for your units."

It's the same order the Devils' ancestors got, the one they still keep. Once Nate has listened to
it, he gets the quest: **report to division headquarters**, which is the 82nd Airborne Division
headquarters on the barracks map, where RonMike stands. The quest marker points there for the
whole first act.

**The Devils in Boston.** A Devils crew has flown the last airworthy **C-130** to Boston to
resupply. Nate runs into them in the street. They tell him about the bird, and he gets on it.

- **Why they're in Boston (idea):** the airport is a graveyard of pre-war aircraft. The Devils keep
  their last plane flying by stripping parts from the wrecks, and trading or scavenging for fuel.
- **Why the signal is only heard in the south end (idea):** the crew carries a relay, or set one up
  where they're working, and it rebroadcasts the old loop. It reaches only that part of the city.
- **The plane is at Boston Airport,** where the Brotherhood of Steel moors the Prydwen.
  **Decided: if the Brotherhood is there, the Devils trade with them.** Not common goods: nothing
  ordinary would stop the Brotherhood taking a working C-130. The Devils pay in **pre-war tech**
  the Brotherhood wants, from the parts of Bragg they can reach: **technical manuals and
  avionics.** The Brotherhood hoards pre-war knowledge, and avionics keep their vertibirds flying.
  - The irony: the Devils, who take a tithe from everyone, pay one of their own to keep flying.
  - A possible hook: the Brotherhood would very much like to know where the Devils' tech comes
    from, and what else is down there. (Mind the scope: a Brotherhood storyline is a lot to add.)
  - Before the Prydwen arrives, or with the Brotherhood gone, the crew works the airport
    undisturbed.
- **The crew can warn him:** "You'll need a rig. Nobody goes out the door without one." That sets
  up the power armor requirement before he boards.

### Getting to Bragg: the jump

The player arrives the way the 82nd does: **by parachute, onto Sicily Drop Zone.**

**Decided: Sicily DZ.** The biggest drop zone on Bragg, and the one even people who know nothing
about airborne operations have heard of. It's the second worldspace.

- **The aircraft** is an interior cell made to look like the inside of a cargo plane in flight:
  a **C-130** or **C-141** (what the 82nd jumped in the early 90s; the C-17 was only being tested
  then). Engine drone, vibration (camera shake), red and green jump lights, static lines and
  anchor cables, jump seats.
- **The jump commands as a scripted scene** with the jumpmaster: "Get ready... Stand up...
  Hook up... Check equipment... Sound off for equipment check... Stand in the door... GO!" Red
  light, then green light.
- **Out the door** is a teleport to a marker about **1,000 ft above Sicily DZ**. The loading screen in between can be its own art.
- **Power armor only.** Power armor takes no fall damage in Fallout 4, so it's the one way to
  survive. The jump only works in power armor: the jumpmaster refuses anyone without it ("You're
  not going out my door without a rig") or, for comedy, lets them go.
- **Landing** can open the story: a raid hits the drop zone as the player comes down, and the
  Devils' reception party fights it off. First impression of both the threat and the protectors.
- **Getting on the plane (decided):** the Devils' last airworthy C-130, at Boston Airport (see
  "The recall" above).

**Technical notes:**
- The drop zones are miles from the barracks area. **Solved by the underground:** the drop zone
  and the barracks area are two separate, small worldspaces joined by the tunnels, so nothing in
  between has to be built.
- At 1,000 ft the player sees the whole map and beyond, so the **LOD and the horizon** (distant
  terrain past the playable edge) have to look right from the air.
- The fall from 1,000 ft (about 305 m, 21,000 game units) takes several seconds in game: long enough
  to see the drop zone, short enough not to bore.
- Script: the door is an activator that checks `IsInPowerArmor()` before moving the player to the
  marker in the air.

### Open questions

- Should the soldiers lean sympathetic, threatening, or truly balanced? (Leaning: balanced, with
  the raids making the soldiers' case for them.)
- Is the hook tied to Nate's past, or is the player a stranger passing through?
- Where does the storm drain come up?
- What exactly is in the armory, and what does opening it do for whoever gets it?
- How does the player get back to Boston afterwards? (The C-130 again?)
- Technical risk to remember: editing the vanilla airport cells can conflict with other mods. A
  plane parked out on the runway, with minimal edits, is safer.
- Who is Top, and do the Devils know the original garrison is still alive underground?
- **Decided: Nate does not relieve the Devils.** Relieving the post happens once, with Top. The
  Devils have made a life at the post over two hundred years; it's their home now, not just a
  mission, and they have nowhere else to go.
- Does the reactor quest come before or after the player picks a side? (Leaning: either, so
  the player can do it while still neutral.)

### Power armor and player settlements

The player arrives in power armor (the jump requires it), but the mod shouldn't be played in it.
**Fusion cores are rare here**, so the player ends up hanging the armor up and walking.

- **Few cores to find:** none or almost none in containers and loot lists in these worldspaces.
  The ones that exist matter.
- **The Devils hoard them.** They live on scarcity, and cores are part of the
  tithe. That makes cores something to trade for, steal, or earn.
- **The catch:** the player can bring a pack of cores from the Commonwealth. **Decided: the
  captain at the drop zone takes them.**
  - The Devils on Sicily DZ are a patrol guarding the underground entrance from raiders. The player
    lands in the middle of a raid, and since raiders are always hostile, the player all but has to
    help the Devils beat them back. Some of the patrol are essential, so **the Devils always win**;
    the question is how well.
  - **Captain Vargas** commands the patrol. When the shooting stops, he comes over to talk, and
    **his mood depends on the fight**: how many raiders the player killed, and how many of his
    patrol he lost.
    - **Heavy losses:** "Well, that was a clusterfuck, and you were the biggest useless fuck in it.
      What are you doing here?"
    - **An overwhelming win:** "Nice shootin', Tex. Now what are you doing in my AO?"
    - **In between:** "Could've been worse. Could've been a lot better. Start talking."
    - Technical: count the player's raider kills (death events on the raiders, checking the killer)
      and the patrol's deaths (only the non-essential members can die), then pick the greeting by
      a quest variable.
  - **Vargas confirms the recall.** The Boston broadcast told Nate to report to division
    headquarters; Vargas is where he learns it's real, that all officers were recalled there, and
    how to get there: through the tunnels.
  - **Then the shakedown.** Vargas has decided he isn't paid enough, so he takes a "fee" from anyone
    who wants into the underground: whatever is worth the most, which for Nate means his fusion
    cores. This is the captain's own racket, not Devils policy; a first, small look at what the
    tithe can turn into.

    > "Things are tough out here. You see what we're up against. And ammo ain't cheap. So... before
    > you go anywhere, you're going to hand over all your power cores."

    (The Devils call fusion cores "power cores".) Three replies:
    1. **"That's highway robbery. Stick a power core up your ass!"** A fight, and **the Devils
       turn hostile at once.** Some or all of the patrol are essential NPCs (they go down but can't
       be killed), so the player can't win it outright, but **the tunnel unlocks** and the player
       can escape. Life gets much harder before the story starts, and two story lines are gone
       (siding with the Devils, and the negotiated peace).
    2. **"I'll have to think about it."** Nothing happens, and **the tunnel stays locked.** The
       player can come back to Vargas and answer again.
    3. **"OK. Here they are."** Vargas takes every core **except the one in the frame**, so the
       player can walk the armor to a settlement and hang it up: **"See... I'm not a monster."**
       Delivered with just the right amount of sarcasm (casting matters for this line). Everybody
       stays happy, and **the tunnel unlocks.**
  - **It can't be skipped:** the underground entrance is the only way off the drop zone.
  - **No spare cores to take** (only the one in the frame, or a clever player who stashed them):
    "Nothing? Hell, you're poorer than we are. Get out of my sight." The tunnel unlocks.
  - **Decided: let the clever player be clever.** A player who drops cores on the DZ before talking
    to him can come back for them later. Anyone set on cheating can spawn cores from the console
    anyway; the shakedown sets the tone, it isn't a lock.
  - **Reporting him:** Nate can report the captain, best of all to the **hardline** leader, who
    has no patience for a soldier lining his own pockets. The captain goes to the stockade, but he
    won't say where the cores are. The player is told plainly: **"We don't have the location of
    your stolen items... yet."** He gives it up near the end of the mod, and the player gets the
    cores back.
  - Or the player can find and steal them back from the post on their own.
  - Also: places where power armor is a bad idea anyway, like tight tunnels a frame won't fit.
- **Two player settlements,** where the player can park the armor and draw settlers in:
  - **One on post.** Where, to decide (an empty company area, a motor pool, the old family
    housing?).
  - **One on Bragg Boulevard,** apart from the settlers' market (the far end of the strip, or by
    the fields?).
  - Each is a normal Fallout 4 workshop settlement (workbench, build area, recruitment beacon).
  - **Consequence:** when the player picks a side, the settlement on the other side's ground
    becomes a target.

## Scope (to keep it finishable)

- **Three small worldspaces:** the barracks area (the map below), Sicily DZ, and Bragg Boulevard
  (the settlers' home, condensed).
- **Interiors:** the aircraft, the headquarters building, the company barracks, the underground
  complex (a few connected cells), and a few storefronts on Bragg Boulevard. Every other barracks
  and shop has a locked or sealed door.
- Four or five quests.
- Two player settlements (one per side of the checkpoint).
- More can be added later.

**Build order**, so there's something playable at every step:
1. The barracks area terrain in the Creation Kit, walkable.
2. One barracks shell, placed on the 1993 layout.
3. The Sicily DZ worldspace and the jump (aircraft, scene, power armor check). It's a complete
   moment on its own, and the most fun thing to show people.
4. The underground, joining the two.
5. Bragg Boulevard: the street, the market, the checkpoint, and the storm drain into the underground.
6. Factions, then quests.

## Building it

### Factions

- **New factions; never edit the Gunners faction.** Copy the Gunners' NPC templates, outfits and
  leveled lists into new records, for the soldiers and the settlers.
- Both start **neutral to the player**. A script on the quest stage where the player picks a side
  makes the other faction an enemy (like the Brotherhood and Institute split in vanilla).
- Restyle the soldiers toward early-90s battle dress: woodland camouflage, the old helmet,
  parachute badges.

### The map

- Area: the 1-504 barracks area as outlined, about 1.2 x 1.9 km, plus 150 m margin:
  **26 x 38 cells** (x -13 to 12, y -19 to 18), true scale.
- Terrain: USGS 3DEP 1 m lidar, 70 to 128 m elevation (57 m of relief).
  `scripts/make_heightmap.py` makes the 16-bit heightmap (`--exaggerate` for taller hills).
- Layout: USGS DOQ from **NAPP photo of 1993-02-23**, 1 m, in `data/doq_raw/`.
  `scripts/make_overlay.py` lines it up with the cell grid; `out/aerial_grid.png` shows each cell's
  Creation Kit coordinates.
- Still to do: find a heightmap importer that works with Fallout 4 and make the script write its
  format.

### Buildings

- **Exterior:** a NIF shell (walls and roof, simple box collision), reused for every identical
  barracks. Split large buildings into a few pieces for occlusion and precombines. A simple LOD
  version for distance (xLODGen).
- **Interior:** a load door (teleport) to a separate interior cell, built from modular kit pieces.
  Room bounds and portals in the Creation Kit are only for occlusion inside big interiors; they don't
  load anything.
- Textures through `.bgsm` material files.
- Tools: **PyNifly** for Blender (version 28.1.0 or later fixed Fallout 4 collision export; re-export
  anything made with earlier versions). Look for the old barracks NIF on the other drives.
