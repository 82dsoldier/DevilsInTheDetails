# The Devils in the Details

Design notes for a Fallout 4 mod set on Fort Bragg, NC, in the 1-504 PIR barracks area as it was in the early 1990s.
Started 2026-10-22. Nothing here is final. The player's choices are drawn as a flowchart in
[DECISION_TREE.md](DECISION_TREE.md); update it when a choice changes here.

## Story

### The premise

Two hundred years after the bombs, the soldiers at Fort Bragg are still there. The garrison's last
order was to **hold the post until relieved**. Nobody came. The descendants kept the order, along with
drill, rank and the regulations, which by now are close to a religion.

They protect the Sandhills from the **raiders and super mutants who attack regularly**. In return
they take a **tithe**: food, water and recruits. Whether that's protection or a racket depends on who
you ask.

**The question the mod asks:** the settlers hate the soldiers, but would they survive without them?

### The soldiers ("the Devils")

- Descendants of the pre-war garrison. Locals call them the **Devils**, after the 504th's WWII
  nickname, "Devils in Baggy Pants", half in fear and half in respect.
- Disciplined, well armed, short of people. Every year they need more food and more recruits
  to hold the line.
- They honestly believe they're the only thing between the settlers and death. They may be
  right.
- They need: someone to show both a decent officer and a hard one. A **reform-minded officer**
  who thinks the tithe has become extortion; a **hardliner** who thinks the settlers owe them
  everything, and who will ask Nate to shell their market (see "The fire mission").

### RonMike, the perfect soldier

**Iron Mike** ("The Airborne Trooper") is the 15-foot statue of a paratrooper unveiled on Bragg in
1961. In the early 90s it stood in the traffic circle between the Fort Bragg Club and the XVIII
Airborne Corps headquarters. It's in the mod for atmosphere, not for a quest.

- **The letters wore off.** Two hundred years of weather took the "I" from the plaque, and what's
  left reads **RON MIKE**. The Devils worship him as **RonMike, the perfect soldier**: the one who
  never broke, never quit and never left his post. The regulations are his scripture.
- **The faith in small things:** recruits are sworn in at his feet; soldiers touch his boot for
  luck before a patrol; the dead are named to him; "RonMike sees you" is both a blessing and a
  warning. Fragments of the worn plaque are recited as a creed, with the gaps filled in by
  guesswork.
- **The sacred text is a technical manual.** The Devils' holiest book, kept at RonMike's shrine, is
  really the instructions for setting up a large radio transmitter. Following it as scripture over
  the generations, the Devils have built the whole thing behind RonMike, mast and all, without
  knowing what it's for. **It has never had power.**
- **The reactor wakes it.** When the player brings the reactor online, the transmitter comes alive
  and starts broadcasting a **numbers station**: groups of four-digit numbers, read out on a loop
  on the Pip-Boy radio. One of those groups is the armory code (see "The MacGuffin").
- **The hardliner and the reformer** read him differently: one as the soldier who obeys, the
  other as the soldier who protects.
- **Top knows better.** The pre-war ghouls remember a statue. "Ron Mike? It's *Iron* Mike, son."
  Whether the player tells the Devils is up to them.
- **Where he stands (decided):** in front of the **82nd Airborne Division headquarters**, in the
  southwest corner of the barracks map. The real traffic circle isn't on the map, so the Devils
  moved him there, all 3,235 pounds, as the centerpiece of their own headquarters. (He was moved
  for real in 1979, so there's precedent.)
- **The plaque text** will be written for the mod; the real inscription doesn't need to be copied.

### Devils slang and fool's errands

Two hundred years on, the Devils still talk like the 82nd, even when nobody remembers what the
words meant. Use these in dialogue and background barks.

- **Sayings:**
  - "Ate up like a soup sandwich." (Nobody's seen a sandwich of any kind in generations.)
  - "You look like a monkey trying to fuck a football." (A drill sergeant's line for a recruit
    fumbling with gear. Fallout 4 is rated M and its raiders swear constantly, so it fits.)
  - "Beat your face, private!" and "Hold down my ground." Both mean: drop and do push-ups.
- **The greeting:** an enlisted soldier passing an officer salutes and says **"All the way, sir!"**
  (the 82nd's motto). The officer returns it with "Airborne!"
  - **A hook for Nate:** once the Devils know he's a commissioned officer, enlisted Devils salute
    him with "All the way, sir!" as he passes: a small, constant reminder of what his credentials
    mean to them. The hardliner's loyal soldiers might pointedly not salute. If the Devils turn
    hostile, the salutes stop.
- **Fool's errands** the old hands send new recruits on, straight out of the motor pool:
  - Go to another shop for a **Yadkin extension**. (Yadkin is the road; there's no such part.)
  - Fetch a **bottle of blinker fluid**.
  - Let the **winter air out of the tires** and fill them with **summer air**.
  - Could be a small, funny side quest: a Devils sergeant sends the player on one. The settlers at
    the Cue and Ale market have been in on the joke for two hundred years and sell "blinker fluid"
    to every recruit who comes looking.
  - Other classics to consider: a box of grid squares, a can of prop wash, the keys to the drop
    zone. (Careful with that last one: it might be how the Key to the Post started.)
- Add more as they come up.

### The settlers

- Survivors from Fayetteville. **Decided: they live on Bragg Boulevard** (see "Bragg Boulevard"
  below), not on farms: survivors gravitate toward what's left of a city, and farmland would be
  boring to build and to walk. Any fields they work are off the map.
- They resent the tithe, which keeps growing; conscription, which takes their children; and the
  curfew and checkpoints.
- They're also raided constantly, and the soldiers are what stops the worst of it. Some know it.
- They need: a **moderate leader** who wants a fairer deal, and a **hard core** who wants the
  soldiers gone whatever the cost. The hard core's **leader** is the settlers' hardliner, the
  mirror of the Devils' one: he's the one who suggests the vent (see the reactor choice).

### Bragg Boulevard: the settlers' home

The strip just outside post is the Fayetteville every soldier from the early 90s remembers: pawn
shops, tattoo parlors, used-car lots, bars, strip malls. It's a **third small worldspace**,
condensed the way Fallout 4 condenses Boston: one stretch of the boulevard, with downtown
Fayetteville as a skyline past the playable edge.

- **The heart of town: the Cue and Ale strip mall.** The Cue and Ale was a strip club in a
  U-shaped strip mall with a huge parking lot: **Sycamore Square Shopping Center**, which also
  had a Food Lion. Today's listings give 3308-3450 Bragg Blvd, around 35.0780 N, 78.9348 W. **It has
  to be in the mod.** The settlers' town grows out of it:
  - **The Cue and Ale itself** is the settlers' bar and gathering place, its sign still up two
    hundred years on. It's where the player meets people, hears rumors and picks up work.
  - **The parking lot is the market:** booths and stalls inside the U, where the settlers sell crops
    from the fields and things they make: light armor, clothes, homemade goods. The settlers aren't
    just people the Devils tax; they're the region's market.
  - **The other storefronts** get whatever names and uses the town needs (a clothier, an armor
    maker, the settler leaders' office). Any we don't want to build collapse into rubble.
  - **What it looks like** (from a recent street photo, kept locally in `data/reference/`, not in
    the repo since it isn't ours): a long, one-story brown brick building across the back of the
    lot, with a **covered walkway of round brick arches** along the storefronts; brick wings on the
    sides; a huge flat lot in front; pines behind; and **two tall lattice radio masts** rising
    behind it. The arches make a good modular kit piece, and the masts are a landmark you can see
    from the freeway and the checkpoint (and maybe where a signal comes from).
  - Still to do: check the mall's 1990s footprint in an aerial photo of the area. The photo is
    from today, so the details may have changed since the 90s.
- **A handful of enterable interiors**, the rest sealed, as on post. The settler leaders live and
  work here, and the codebook is kept here.
- **The Devils' checkpoint.** Bragg was an open post in the 90s, with no gates. The Devils built a
  makeshift one: wrecked cars, sandbags, a guard shack across the road into town. That's where
  the tithe, the curfew and the checkpoints from the settlers' complaints become something the
  player walks through. **Decided: on Yadkin Road.** The All-American Freeway runs parallel to
  Bragg Blvd and never meets it; Yadkin meets Bragg Blvd at a Y intersection (where the McDonald's
  was). The checkpoint sits at the map edge on Yadkin, and the stretch of Yadkin between post and
  town is condensed out.
- **The area (decided):** a four-sided outline taking in the end of Yadkin (the way in), a stretch
  of the All-American Freeway (a freeway, like Boston's), and part of Bragg Blvd:
  - 35°04'56.60"N 78°58'10.73"W
  - 35°04'11.10"N 78°56'05.25"W
  - 35°04'46.18"N 78°55'46.34"W
  - 35°05'42.02"N 78°57'10.86"W

  The southeast edge was moved out 400 m from the first draft, which put Sycamore Square right on
  the edge. About 3.5 x 2.1 km at its longest, **4.9 km² (about 1,420 cells)**, a little bigger
  than the barracks map. The worldspace grid runs north-south, so the box around it, with the same
  150 m margin as the barracks map, is **68 x 53 cells**; the corners outside the outline are
  non-playable margin. Heightmap: `make_heightmap.py --area bragg_blvd` (43 to 76 m, 32 m of
  relief, 2261 game units top to bottom). (The first outline, 12.7 km² along Bragg
  Blvd, was nearly four times the barracks map and was dropped.)
- **Detail where it counts:** the strip around the Y gets the work. The freeway is mostly road
  pieces and wrecks, and the **wooded areas become the settlers' fields**: visible from the
  street and the freeway, but not built for exploring.
- **Two ways in:** the road through the Devils' checkpoint (a map-edge transition to the barracks
  area), and a storm drain branch of the underground that comes up in town. The drains are how the
  settlers smuggle around the tithe, and how the raiders get in.
- **The vents are on post,** so venting the hot zone never touches the town.

### Raiders and super mutants

Not a side to choose: a constant pressure. Regular attacks on the farms and the post are what make
the soldiers necessary, and what make the player's choice cost something. Whatever the ending,
the raids continue: whoever is left has to deal with them.

- **The super mutants nest at the vents.** Even with the fans dead, a little radiation seeps up
  through the outer vents on post. Super mutants are immune to it, and nobody else wants to stand
  there, so the ground around the vents is theirs: a nest right inside the Devils' own area that
  they've never been able to clear. It's where the mutant attacks come from. (The raiders come up
  through the tunnels from the east.)
- **The reactor choice touches them too.** Venting without filters kills Devils and leaves the
  mutants untouched: the dark ending gets darker. Fixing the filters first doesn't remove the
  mutants, but the ground around the vents stops being worth more to them than anywhere else.

### The player's hook

**Nate is a pre-war Army veteran: the only living person with valid 2077 military credentials.**

- The **armory**, deep in the underground complex, still recognizes an active-duty officer's ID.
  Suddenly both sides need the player.
- **But the ID is only half of it** (see "The MacGuffin" below): the armory works on the
  two-person rule, and the other half is held by the factions themselves.
- Unlocking it for the soldiers makes them unstoppable. Giving it to the settlers flips the
  balance. Or the player can destroy it.
- **Why go to North Carolina:** a 2077 emergency broadcast, picked up in Boston (see "The
  recall" below).

### The MacGuffin: the Key to the Post and the codebook

Real weapons storage used the **two-person rule**: nobody opens it alone. The armory needs
**a valid officer's ID (Nate) plus a 2077 authenticator in two parts**, and each faction holds one
part without knowing what it is.

- **The Key to the Post (the Devils have it).** For two hundred years, every commander of the
  Devils has worn a battered metal key on a chain as the badge of office, handed over at every
  change of command. It's sacred to them, and nobody remembers what it opens.
- **The codebook (the settlers have it).** A sealed authentication codebook: the day's code words,
  like the real-world "cookie" authenticators. A scavenger pulled it out of a pre-war office
  generations ago; the settlers keep it as a curiosity, or close to a holy text. Perhaps the
  moderate settler leader is its keeper.
- **The numbers station (Devils' transmitter).** Once the reactor is on, the transmitter behind
  RonMike broadcasts groups of four-digit numbers, and one group is the code that opens the
  armory along with the key. **Decided:** the codebook is how you know *which* group. Without it,
  the broadcast is just numbers. That's how real numbers stations worked (useless without the
  matching codebook), and it keeps the settlers' half of the two-person rule. So the armory needs
  four things: **Nate's ID, the Key to the Post, the code from the broadcast, and the codebook to
  find it**, which means the reactor has to be on, too.
- **How the player gets both is the choice:**
  - **With the Devils:** they hand over the key; the codebook has to be earned (lighting up the
    Cue and Ale), stolen or taken by force from the settlers.
  - **With the settlers:** the player takes the key from the Devils' commander. To the Devils
    that's about the worst insult there is.
  - **Peace:** both leaders bring their halves to the armory door together. The final scene.
- **The way in is its own problem.** Even with the ID, the key and the codebook, nobody can reach
  the armory door: it sits in a radiation hot zone swarming with feral ghouls. Getting there means
  restarting the reactor (see "The hot zone and the reactor" below), a separate quest that can be
  done before or after the player picks a side.

### The underground

Not realistic, and doesn't need to be: this is Fallout, and every pre-war base had a secret.

- **A tunnel complex under the post**, with an entrance in each of the two exterior areas: one near
  the drop zone (found early, since the player lands near it) and one in the barracks area (under
  the headquarters building or a motor pool, perhaps).
- **Its pre-war cover story:** officially a utility tunnel, an emergency command bunker or
  munitions storage. Terminals and holotapes reveal what it really was.
- **It explains the raids:** the raiders and mutants get past the Devils' patrols through the
  tunnels. Sealing them, or leaving them open, can be part of the ending.
- **Who's down there:** feral ghouls who were the pre-war garrison, and a few who never went
  feral (see below); automated defenses still following 2077 orders; settlers using the storm
  drain branch to Bragg Boulevard to smuggle goods around the tithe.
- **Shape: three ways from the drop zone entrance.** Past Vargas's patrol, the tunnels split:
  - **East: the raiders.** A raider camp, with their own version of a guard gate across the
    tunnel. This is the raiders' road onto the post.
  - **West: the ghouls.** Top's squad, and past them the heavily irradiated hot zone and the
    armory at its heart. The player can meet Top early, before ever reaching Bragg.
  - **North: Bragg.** The way out into the barracks area, and the quest marker for division
    headquarters.
  - **Still to place:** the reactor, and the storm drain branch to Bragg Boulevard. Idea: both lie
    **east, past the raider gate.** Top already says the way to the reactor runs through
    raider-held tunnels, and a raider-held drain into town is how the raiders reach the settlers.
  - All of it: utility tunnels, pipes, steam, flooded sections.

### The hot zone and the reactor

**The armory sits behind a wall of radiation, and the radiation is full of feral ghouls.**

- **The armory complex goes down several stories**, with a **nuclear weapons storage vault at the
  bottom.** (Real precedent: in the Cold War, airborne engineers and special forces trained with
  small atomic demolition munitions, nukes light enough to jump with.)
- **Why it's hot:** when the bombs fell, the vault's containment failed, the reactor scrammed and the
  exhaust fans stopped. What leaked from the vault has hung in the dead air ever since, hottest at
  the bottom and rising up through the levels.
- **Going down:** the levels are joined by elevators and stairwells. The elevators are dead until
  the reactor is back on (restoring them is already one of the things the reactor does).
- **Venting clears the air, not the source.** After the fans run, the upper levels are safe, but
  the vault at the bottom is still dangerous: a last, optional descent for anyone who wants to see
  what's down there.
- **The ferals** are what's left of the pre-war garrison: the soldiers who stayed at their posts
  as the air went bad. They still crowd the corridors around the armory door, which is why nobody
  has looted it in two hundred years.
- **The ghouls who didn't turn.** Living at the edge of the hot zone, among and behind the ferals,
  is a small squad of non-feral ghouls: **the original 2077 garrison**, the soldiers the Devils are
  descended from, or their comrades. They got the same order: hold the post until relieved.
  - **Their leader** is a pre-war NCO (a first sergeant, "Top", fits: the one who kept the unit
    together). He tells the player plainly: **nobody gets to that armory until the power's back on.
    The fans have to run to vent the air.**
  - **Why they can't do it themselves:** they're too few, the way to the reactor runs through
    raider-held tunnels, and the reactor controls want an officer's authorization (Nate again).
    Perhaps also: Top believes the hot air is what turned his people feral, and every year he stays
    by the armory he loses another one.
  - **How they treat the ferals:** as their own soldiers. Top doesn't want them killed for sport,
    but once the air clears, "put them down clean" is the last order he gives about them, and his
    squad fights beside the player.
  - **What they think of the Devils and the settlers:** a third view on the mod's question. They
    remember the post before the Devils became what they are. They may never have gone up to see.
- **Restarting the reactor** is a dungeon of its own: down the reactor branch, past raiders and
  dormant defenses, to bring the reactor online. Doing so:
  1. Starts the exhaust fans. The hot zone clears over a short timed sequence (sirens, fans spinning
     up, the green haze thinning).
  2. Wakes the automated defenses, which still follow 2077 orders and don't know who anyone is.
     Among them, an **artillery battery** still tied into 2077 fire control (see "The fire
     mission" below).
  3. Restores light and power through the complex (doors, elevators, terminals).
  4. **Powers the post.** The reactor was the post's power plant, so the lights come back on in the
     barracks area too. The Devils gain from it whoever turns it on. (Real precedent: the Army ran
     small reactors at posts and remote sites; SM-1 at Fort Belvoir fed the post's grid from 1957.)
- **The choice at the reactor: vent now, or fix the filters first.** **Decided:** the exhaust vents
  come out **on post, among the Devils' barracks**, disguised as something ordinary (a boiler plant
  stack or a building's ventilation). The filters that should scrub the air failed long ago. At the
  reactor the player decides:
  - **Turn it on now.** The hot zone clears at once, and the poisoned air pours out over the
    barracks. A lot of Devils die, and the Devils turn hostile for good. A player siding with the
    settlers might do it on purpose, as a weapon: the darkest way to win.
  - **Fix the filters first.** Slower, and nobody dies. An optional objective in the reactor quest:
    1. **Pull an old filter from one of the vent stacks on post.** The stacks are the super
       mutants' nest, so this means a fight.
    2. **Find someone who can copy it.** The quest leads Nate to the **settlers' hardliner**, who
       knows the man for the job, and who uses the moment to plant the idea:
       > "I hear you're trying to restart the reactor. It'd be a shame if the filters got left out
       > and all those soldiers got killed."
       He makes the introduction anyway. The choice stays Nate's.
    3. **The tinker on Bragg Boulevard** copies it: 3 or 4 new filters, one per stack.
    4. **What the tinker needs** (to decide). Fetch quests can feel like chores, but Nate needs
       reasons to explore. A middle way: common parts the player can buy or scrap from their own
       junk (Fallout 4 components like aluminum, adhesive, fiberglass), plus **one or two special
       parts that only come from places worth visiting anyway**, each with something to find
       there (a holotape, a character, a piece of the story). Candidates: the raider camp east in
       the tunnels, the motor pool, the drop zone.
    5. Install them, then start the reactor. (Where: in a filter room near the reactor, so it's one
       trip, or at each stack topside, which means more mutant fights. Leaning: near the reactor.)
    - **If the settlers are hostile,** the tinker won't help. **Decided:** the Devils' **armorer**
      can make the filters instead, at a price.
- **Ending Top's story: Nate relieves the post.** Once the way is clear, Nate, the only officer
  with valid credentials, formally relieves Top and his squad. Two hundred years after the order,
  someone finally came. This ends the hot zone quest and opens the way to the armory.
- **Top and his squad move up to Bragg.** **Decided:** they become the **first
  settlers of the player's settlement on post**, which gives it its first residents and
  defenders. And the Devils' reaction is a scene in itself: ghouls who served with their
  ancestors, walking out of the ground. Saints of RonMike, or monsters?

**Technical notes:**
- **The player always has power armor** (it's required for the jump), and power armor resists
  radiation. Rads alone won't stop them. The block needs to be a hard one: a **radiation interlock**
  on the blast door into the hot zone that won't open until the air reading is safe, with the
  radiation high enough that a player who gets past it some other way still can't survive.
- Radiation as hazard trigger volumes in the hot zone, disabled by a script on the quest stage that
  starts the fans. Fans as animated activators, switched on by the same stage.
- The ferals stay after the air clears; only the radiation goes.

### The numbers station quest

When Nate climbs out of the tunnels with the reactor running, the Pip-Boy picks up the transmitter
behind RonMike: **groups of four-digit numbers, on a loop.** Hearing it starts a quest: **find out
how to decode it.**

- **The Devils think it's a miracle.** Their sacred transmitter, silent for two hundred years, has
  started to speak: RonMike's voice. Nobody can understand it, and everybody has a theory. **Nate
  made RonMike speak, and that pushes the Devils hard toward friendly.**
- **Top recognizes it.** "That's a numbers station. Useless without the book." The
  authentication codebooks were kept on post before the war; he points Nate toward where it
  should have been, and that trail leads to the settlers, who have had it for generations.
- From there the quest becomes getting the codebook: earned by lighting up the Cue and Ale (below),
  or stolen or taken from the settlers, or brought to the door by their leader in the peace ending.

### Lighting up the Cue and Ale

**The settlers' answer to RonMike speaking:** something they've believed impossible all their lives,
happening in front of them. It pushes the settlers as hard toward friendly as RonMike's voice pushes
the Devils.

- **The quest:** the tinker says the old **Food Lion** has its own pre-war backup generator, dead
  for two hundred years. Nate gathers the parts, clears whatever has nested in the loading dock,
  and brings it back online.
- **The scene:** the **Cue and Ale sign flickers on** over the market for the first time in two
  centuries, and the market goes quiet.
- **What it does for them:** the Food Lion's cold storage runs again, so the harvest keeps. Food
  that keeps means less hunger and less need for the Devils: straight at the question the mod asks.
- **Its own generator, not the reactor's power:** a line down Yadkin from the post would make the
  settlers depend on the Devils, who could cut it whenever the tithe was late. Power of their own
  is independence, which is what the settlers really want.
- **The reward: the codebook.** In thanks, the settlers give Nate their holy text, the codebook,
  which is what decodes the numbers station. Freely given, which makes it harder if Nate later
  sides against them.
- **The balance:** RonMike speaks for every player, since everyone restarts the reactor. The
  Cue and Ale has to be sought out. That's deliberate: the Devils hold the power, and the
  settlers have to be earned.

### Side quests

Each side needs side quests that tilt it toward Nate. Not every one has to cost him with the other
side, but **some should**: helping one side often means taking from the other. **No escort
quests.** (Seeds below; the conversations and details are still to write.)

**For the settlers:**
- **Clear the raiders from the smuggling routes.** The storm drains are how the settlers move goods
  around the tithe, and raiders hold them. Clearing them helps the settlers, and costs the Devils
  their tithe: the Devils turn against Nate if they find out.
- **Bring a conscript home** (below). Costs him with the Devils if done badly.
- **The market thief.** Someone has been stealing from the booths at the Cue and Ale. The trail
  leads into the storm drains, to **a Devils deserter** hiding there and stealing to stay alive.
  (Idea for who it is; to settle.) What Nate does with him:
  - **Hand him to the settlers.** They get justice, and their goods back. Settlers approve.
  - **Turn him over to the Devils.** Desertion is about the worst crime there is to them. Devils
    approve; the settlers got their thief caught, so they're satisfied too.
  - **Help him disappear.** He's out of the market for good and the settlers are happy, but if
    the Devils find out Nate hid a deserter, they turn on him.
- **The poisoned water.** The settlers' water has gone bad, and everyone blames the Devils.
  **A mystery, not a repair job** (so it doesn't play like the Food Lion generator): Nate follows
  the clues to find out what happened.
  - **The water:** purifiers on the **Cape Fear River**, piped into town. The river is about 5 km
    east of the Bragg Boulevard map, so it stays off the map: the quest happens at the **pump house**
    where the pipe comes in, plus a small separate cell for the **intake on the river**. (There are
    no creeks in the area to use instead.)
  - **The truth: the settlers' hardliner sabotaged the purifiers himself, to frame the Devils.** He
    wants a revolt, and nothing starts one like believing the soldiers poisoned your water. The
    mirror of the Devils' hardliner asking for the fire mission.
  - **The choice:**
    - **Expose him.** The hard core loses its grip, the Devils are cleared, and peace gets closer.
      Settlers and Devils both approve, though the hardliner's people won't forget.
    - **Keep quiet.** The water gets fixed, the anger at the Devils stays, and it works for a
      player siding with the settlers. The Devils lean against Nate if they ever learn he knew.
  - Either way, Nate gets the purifiers running again, and the settlers have clean water.

**For the Devils:**
- **Go out with a tithe patrol** to collect from the market. The Devils' mirror of the smuggling
  quest: the Devils approve, the settlers remember who came with the soldiers.
- **Clear the super mutant nest at the vents.** The nest the Devils have never been able to clear.
  Pure gain (and easier once the filters are in).
- **Find a missing patrol** in the tunnels, toward the raider camp in the east. Pure gain.
- **Report Vargas** (see the shakedown). The hardliner approves.
- **A fool's errand:** a sergeant sends Nate for blinker fluid (see the slang). Small and funny,
  and the settlers sell him a bottle.

#### Bringing a conscript home

A settler family's son was taken in the conscription. As an officer, Nate can sign a discharge.

- **Done badly,** he just signs the papers and walks the boy out, and the Devils take it as an
  officer undercutting them: they lean toward hostility.
- **Done right,** he goes through the chain of command: the reform officer's approval, or a
  **volunteer found to take the boy's place.** That's a small preview of the peace ending's terms
  (volunteers instead of conscription), and nobody loses face.
- Either way, the family and the settlers warm to Nate.


### Story shape

1. **Arrival.** The jump; the raid on the drop zone; Vargas and the shakedown. Both sides are
   neutral. Small jobs for each; learn how they see things. Witness a raid, and see what the
   soldiers do (and what they charge for it).
   - **An explorer's path through the first act:** from the drop zone, west to Top; the reactor
     quest (and the filters, if Nate bothers); relieve Top; then north out of the tunnels into
     Bragg, where the numbers station is already on the air. A player who goes straight north
     reaches Bragg first and does the reactor later; both orders have to work.
2. **Rising tension.** Something forces the issue: a tithe raid on a farm, an ambush on a patrol,
   a missing recruit. The player learns what the armory is, and that it needs more than an ID.
3. **The choice.** Help one side; **the other becomes hostile**. Get the key and the codebook.
4. **The ending.** The armory decides who wins, or how it ends.
5. **Optional third path.** Speech checks bring the reform officer and the moderate settler leader
   together for a negotiated peace: a fairer tithe, volunteers instead of conscription, and both
   halves of the authenticator brought to the door together.
   - **Speech checks earned along the way** (how Nate brings them together is still mostly open):
     - **Volunteers instead of conscription:** unlocked if Nate brought the conscript home **the
       right way** (a volunteer took the boy's place, or the reform officer approved it). He's
       already shown the reformer that volunteers can work, and the settlers that the Devils can
       bend. Done badly, or not at all, and the check isn't there.
     - More to find: the other peace terms (a fairer tithe, and whatever else) should each have
       a check earned the same way, by something Nate did earlier.

### Consequences to show

- **Side with the soldiers:** the settlers are safe from raids and live under the Devils for good.
  Hardliner or reformer in charge changes how that feels.
- **Side with the settlers:** they're free, and the raids get worse. Can they hold? Maybe the
  player has to help them build defenses, or maybe some farms are lost.
- **Negotiated peace:** the raids continue, but now the two sides face them together.
- **Destroy the armory:** nobody gets it, and **both the Devils and the settlers turn hostile** to
  the player. Each side wanted it; the player took it from both. The raids continue, with no side
  holding the advantage, and the player is on their own.

### Rewards

The choices have consequences; these are what Nate gets for them.

- **Every ending: the armory's contents.** Pre-war 82nd gear: weapons, ammo, and **fusion cores**
  (which finally matter, since cores are scarce); woodland BDUs, a maroon beret, jump wings. And an
  **"Airborne" perk**: reduced fall damage outside power armor.
- **One signature reward per ending:**
  - **The Devils: artillery on call.** Throw a smoke grenade and the Devils' battery fires, like
    the Minutemen's artillery in vanilla. The guns Nate could have turned on the settlers now work
    for him.
  - **The settlers: the market opens up.** Better prices, rare stock, and a unique armor the
    tinker makes for him.
  - **Peace: the C-130 is his ride.** Travel between Boston and Bragg whenever he wants. The
    hardest ending gets the best reward.
  - **Destroy: loot it all first, then blow it.** The biggest haul of any ending: no friends left,
    but the most raw power. If the vault still holds nukes, that includes one **small atomic
    demolition munition**: a backpack nuke, a unique Fat Man variant.
- **Relieving Top: his pearl-handled 1911.** The pistol he's carried for two hundred years, a gift
  from his first commander, engraved to him and signed. He hands it to the officer who finally
  relieved him. (Who the commander was, and the exact inscription, to write.)
  - Technical: Fallout 4 has no 1911. A new model on the 10mm pistol's animations (see
    "Weapons"), or to start, a retextured, renamed vanilla pistol with pearl grips.
- **Getting back to Boston, in every ending.** Only the peace ending gets the plane on demand, but
  every player needs a way home, or a player who sided with the settlers is stuck. **Decided: the
  C-130 aircrew stay out of the feud.** Pilots are too rare to risk, so they fly anyone who pays, even
  someone the Devils want dead. Peace makes it free and whenever Nate likes.
- **Where the plane lives: the assault strip on Sicily DZ.** Sicily has a dirt landing strip
  built for C-130s, so the bird is based there, and that's where Nate finds it to fly home (or,
  in the peace ending, whenever he likes). It's also part of why Vargas's patrol is on Sicily:
  they guard the strip and the last plane, not just the tunnel.
  - **The catch:** a player at war with the Devils has to get past that patrol to reach the
    plane. Idea: **the strip is neutral ground.** Nobody fires near the last airworthy plane, since
    one stray round could ground it for good. Inside the strip's boundary, even hostile Devils hold
    their fire, and the player had better hold theirs.
  - Technical: the strip is just flat dirt in the Sicily DZ worldspace, cheap to build. The plane
    is a static model on the ground, with an activator for the flight (the aircraft interior, then
    a teleport to Boston).
- **Arriving in Boston: two choices on board.**
  1. **Land at Boston Airport** (the default, no jump). Where the plane goes anyway, and the way
     home for a player who left the power armor hanging at Bragg. A player at war with the
     Brotherhood lands in their territory, and that's their problem.
  2. **Jump into Sanctuary Hills** (power armor only). Nate comes home by parachute, over his old
     neighborhood, where the story started. It mirrors the jump onto Sicily. Sanctuary works
     because every player has been there, almost every player has made it a settlement (a
     workbench and a power armor station to land beside), and the bridge and the river make a
     clear target from 1,000 ft.
  - Technical: no edits to Boston's cells. The script moves the player to a point high above an
    existing vanilla reference (Sanctuary's map marker) instead of placing a new marker in a vanilla
    cell, which is what would conflict with other mods.
  - Considered and dropped: jumping onto any settlement the player owns. Every settlement would
    need checking for a safe landing, and it would feel like fast travel instead of coming home.

### The recall: how it starts

**The broadcast.** Wandering toward the south end of Boston, Nate picks up a radio signal, a
2077 recording still on its loop:

> "This is not a drill. The United States has been hit by nuclear weapons. Everyone is ordered to
> stand their post until relieved or until further orders arrive. All officers are required to
> report to division headquarters to get assignments for your units."

It's the same order the Devils' ancestors got, the one they still keep. Once Nate has listened to
it, he gets the quest: **report to division headquarters**, which is the 82nd Airborne Division
headquarters on the barracks map, where RonMike stands. The quest marker points there for the
whole first act.

**The Devils in Boston.** A Devils crew has flown the last airworthy **C-130** to Boston to
resupply. Nate runs into them in the street. They tell him about the bird, and he gets on it.

- **Why they're in Boston (idea):** the airport is a graveyard of pre-war aircraft. The Devils keep
  their last plane flying by stripping parts from the wrecks, and trading or scavenging for fuel.
- **Why the signal is only heard in the south end (idea):** the crew carries a relay, or set one up
  where they're working, and it rebroadcasts the old loop. It reaches only that part of the city.
- **The plane is at Boston Airport,** where the Brotherhood of Steel moors the Prydwen.
  **Decided: if the Brotherhood is there, the Devils trade with them.** Not common goods: nothing
  ordinary would stop the Brotherhood taking a working C-130. The Devils pay in **pre-war tech**
  the Brotherhood wants, from the parts of Bragg they can reach: **technical manuals and
  avionics.** The Brotherhood hoards pre-war knowledge, and avionics keep their vertibirds flying.
  - The irony: the Devils, who take a tithe from everyone, pay one of their own to keep flying.
  - A possible hook: the Brotherhood would very much like to know where the Devils' tech comes
    from, and what else is down there. (Mind the scope: a Brotherhood storyline is a lot to add.)
  - Before the Prydwen arrives, or with the Brotherhood gone, the crew works the airport
    undisturbed.
- **The crew can warn him:** "You'll need a rig. Nobody goes out the door without one." That sets
  up the power armor requirement before he boards.

### Getting to Bragg: the jump

The player arrives the way the 82nd does: **by parachute, onto Sicily Drop Zone.**

**Decided: Sicily DZ.** The biggest drop zone on Bragg, and the one even people who know nothing
about airborne operations have heard of. It's the second worldspace.

- **The area (decided):** a four-sided outline about 2.2 km on a side, around the landing ground and
  the assault strip:
  - 35°08'57.04"N 79°08'53.59"W
  - 35°10'03.34"N 79°08'26.20"W
  - 35°09'47.29"N 79°07'09.71"W
  - 35°08'39.13"N 79°07'31.10"W

  **4.5 km² (about 1,310 cells)**, about the size of Bragg Boulevard; with the 150 m margin the box
  is **50 x 50 cells**. Heightmap: `make_heightmap.py --area sicily` (50 to 105 m, 55 m of relief,
  3817 game units top to bottom). Mostly open ground, so cheaper to dress than the other two. (A
  first outline, 12.4 km² and 75 x 96 cells, was more than the other two maps together and was
  cut down.)
- **The assault strip:** along the northwest side of the field, running southwest to northeast
  (heading about 45°), cells about (-16, -10) to (4, 4):
  - 35°09'03.38"N 79°08'38.80"W
  - 35°09'33.48"N 79°08'01.32"W
  - 35°09'29.27"N 79°07'51.55"W
  - 35°08'55.66"N 79°08'30.04"W

  About 1,370 m long (4,500 ft) and 300 m across, counting the cleared ground on each side; the
  dirt runway itself is a narrower band down the middle, darker in the 1993 photo. It falls about
  11 m from the southwest end to the northeast (under 1%), so it can follow the ground as it is.
- **The staging area:** a sand parking lot with bleachers, on the west edge of the field beside the
  southwest end of the strip, against the tree line, cells about (-17, -7) to (-11, 0):
  - 35°09'23.59"N 79°08'32.58"W
  - 35°09'15.20"N 79°08'27.11"W
  - 35°09'10.30"N 79°08'33.23"W
  - 35°09'19.46"N 79°08'39.87"W

  About 220 x 300 m. A road comes in from the west through the trees. Here the plane parks off the
  strip, the aircrew keeps its camp, and Vargas's patrol holds the ground; the bleachers, where
  families once watched the jumps, make a ready-made firing line in the raider fight.
- **The tunnel entrance:** in the trees behind the bleachers, on the side away from the field (the
  west side of the lot, around cells (-17, -3) to (-16, -1); the exact spot is set when the
  bleachers are placed). The patrol guards it from the lot, and the bleachers screen it from the
  field, so a player who lands out on the drop zone can't see it until the fight leads them there.

- **The aircraft** is an interior cell made to look like the inside of a cargo plane in flight:
  a **C-130** or **C-141** (what the 82nd jumped in the early 90s; the C-17 was only being tested
  then). Engine drone, vibration (camera shake), red and green jump lights, static lines and
  anchor cables, jump seats.
- **The jump commands as a scripted scene** with the jumpmaster: "Get ready... Stand up...
  Hook up... Check equipment... Sound off for equipment check... Stand in the door... GO!" Red
  light, then green light.
- **Out the door** is a teleport to a marker about **1,000 ft above Sicily DZ**. The loading screen in between can be its own art.
- **Power armor only.** Power armor takes no fall damage in Fallout 4, so it's the one way to
  survive. The jump only works in power armor: the jumpmaster refuses anyone without it ("You're
  not going out my door without a rig") or, for comedy, lets them go.
- **Landing** can open the story: a raid hits the drop zone as the player comes down, and the
  Devils' reception party fights it off. First impression of both the threat and the protectors.
- **Getting on the plane (decided):** the Devils' last airworthy C-130, at Boston Airport (see
  "The recall" above).

**Technical notes:**
- The drop zones are miles from the barracks area. **Solved by the underground:** the drop zone
  and the barracks area are two separate, small worldspaces joined by the tunnels, so nothing in
  between has to be built.
- At 1,000 ft the player sees the whole map and beyond, so the **LOD and the horizon** (distant
  terrain past the playable edge) have to look right from the air.
- The fall from 1,000 ft (about 305 m, 21,000 game units) takes several seconds in game: long enough
  to see the drop zone, short enough not to bore.
- Script: the door is an activator that checks `IsInPowerArmor()` before moving the player to the
  marker in the air.

- Should the soldiers lean sympathetic, threatening, or truly balanced? (Leaning: balanced, with
  the raids making the soldiers' case for them.)
- Is the hook tied to Nate's past, or is the player a stranger passing through?
- Where does the storm drain come up?
- What exactly is in the armory, and what does opening it do for whoever gets it? **Does the vault

- Does the reactor quest come before or after the player picks a side? (Leaning: either, so
  the player can do it while still neutral.)

### The fire mission

The vent is the dark way to break the Devils. **The fire mission is the dark way to break the
settlers**, so neither side is the only one the player can do the unthinkable to.

- **The guns:** Bragg is an artillery post, and the 82nd had its own artillery. When the reactor
  wakes the post's defenses, a battery of guns comes back online, still tied into 2077 fire
  control. **Firing them takes an officer's authorization**, and Nate is the only one alive.
- **The ask:** the **hardliner** asks Nate to authorize a fire mission on the **Cue and Ale
  market**, to break the settlers' resistance for good. It's his defining moment, and saying yes is
  the clear-cut choice: **I'm siding with this hardass.**
- **If Nate authorizes it:** the market is shelled, many settlers die, and **the settlers turn
  hostile for good.** The market and its booths are left in ruins. (Mind the player settlement on
  Bragg Boulevard: in the blast zone, or near enough to feel it.)
- **The mirror of the vent:** both are dark acts the reactor makes possible, each one kills one
  side, and **each is suggested by the other side's hardliner**: the settlers' hardliner hints at
  leaving the filters out, the Devils' hardliner asks for the fire mission. The vent can still
  happen out of neglect; the fire mission is always deliberate, and it's the one place Nate's
  credentials are used to kill rather than to open a door.
- Slang tie-in (idea): "a box of grid squares" is what the Devils call a fire mission.
- Technical: scripted explosions on markers around the market (vanilla Fallout 4 already does
  artillery strikes this way), then swap the market for a ruined version with an enable parent and
  kill or remove the settlers who were there.

### Power armor and player settlements

The player arrives in power armor (the jump requires it), but the mod shouldn't be played in it.
**Fusion cores are rare here**, so the player ends up hanging the armor up and walking.

- **Few cores to find:** none or almost none in containers and loot lists in these worldspaces.
  The ones that exist matter.
- **The Devils hoard them.** They live on scarcity, and cores are part of the
  tithe. That makes cores something to trade for, steal, or earn.
- **The catch:** the player can bring a pack of cores from the Commonwealth. **Decided: the
  captain at the drop zone takes them.**
  - The Devils on Sicily DZ are a patrol guarding the underground entrance from raiders. The player
    lands in the middle of a raid, and since raiders are always hostile, the player all but has to
    help the Devils beat them back. Some of the patrol are essential, so **the Devils always win**;
    the question is how well.
  - **Captain Vargas** commands the patrol. When the shooting stops, he comes over to talk, and
    **his mood depends on the fight**: how many raiders the player killed, and how many of his
    patrol he lost.
    - **Heavy losses:** "Well, that was a clusterfuck, and you were the biggest useless fuck in it.
      What are you doing here?"
    - **An overwhelming win:** "Nice shootin', Tex. Now what are you doing in my AO?"
    - **In between:** "Could've been worse. Could've been a lot better. Start talking."
    - Technical: count the player's raider kills (death events on the raiders, checking the killer)
      and the patrol's deaths (only the non-essential members can die), then pick the greeting by
      a quest variable.
  - **Vargas confirms the recall.** The Boston broadcast told Nate to report to division
    headquarters; Vargas is where he learns it's real, that all officers were recalled there, and
    how to get there: through the tunnels.
  - **Then the shakedown.** Vargas has decided he isn't paid enough, so he takes a "fee" from anyone
    who wants into the underground: whatever is worth the most, which for Nate means his fusion
    cores. This is the captain's own racket, not Devils policy; a first, small look at what the
    tithe can turn into.

    > "Things are tough out here. You see what we're up against. And ammo ain't cheap. So... before
    > you go anywhere, you're going to hand over all your power cores."

    (The Devils call fusion cores "power cores".) Three replies:
    1. **"That's highway robbery. Stick a power core up your ass!"** A fight, and **the Devils
       turn hostile at once.** Some or all of the patrol are essential NPCs (they go down but can't
       be killed), so the player can't win it outright, but **the tunnel unlocks** and the player
       can escape. Life gets much harder before the story starts, and two story lines are gone
       (siding with the Devils, and the negotiated peace).
    2. **"I'll have to think about it."** Nothing happens, and **the tunnel stays locked.** The
       player can come back to Vargas and answer again.
    3. **"OK. Here they are."** Vargas takes every core **except the one in the frame**, so the
       player can walk the armor to a settlement and hang it up: **"See... I'm not a monster."**
       Delivered with just the right amount of sarcasm (casting matters for this line). Everybody
       stays happy, and **the tunnel unlocks.**
  - **It can't be skipped:** the underground entrance is the only way off the drop zone.
  - **No spare cores to take** (only the one in the frame, or a clever player who stashed them):
    "Nothing? Hell, you're poorer than we are. Get out of my sight." The tunnel unlocks.
  - **Decided: let the clever player be clever.** A player who drops cores on the DZ before talking
    to him can come back for them later. Anyone set on cheating can spawn cores from the console
    anyway; the shakedown sets the tone, it isn't a lock.
  - **Reporting him:** Nate can report the captain, best of all to the **hardline** leader, who
    has no patience for a soldier lining his own pockets. The captain goes to the stockade, but he
    won't say where the cores are. The player is told plainly: **"We don't have the location of
    your stolen items... yet."** He gives it up near the end of the mod, and the player gets the
    cores back.
  - Or the player can find and steal them back from the post on their own.
  - Also: places where power armor is a bad idea anyway, like tight tunnels a frame won't fit.
- **Two player settlements,** where the player can park the armor and draw settlers in:
  - **One on post.** Where, to decide (an empty company area, a motor pool, the old family
    housing?).
  - **One on Bragg Boulevard,** apart from the settlers' market (the far end of the strip, or by
    the fields?).
  - Each is a normal Fallout 4 workshop settlement (workbench, build area, recruitment beacon).
  - **Consequence:** when the player picks a side, the settlement on the other side's ground
    becomes a target.

## Scope (to keep it finishable)

- **Three small worldspaces:** the barracks area (the map below), Sicily DZ, and Bragg Boulevard
  (the settlers' home, condensed).
- **Interiors:** the aircraft, the headquarters building, the company barracks, the underground
  complex (a few connected cells), and a few storefronts on Bragg Boulevard. Every other barracks
  and shop has a locked or sealed door.
- Four or five quests.
- Two player settlements (one per side of the checkpoint).
- More can be added later.

**Build order**, so there's something playable at every step:
1. The barracks area terrain in the Creation Kit, walkable.
2. One barracks shell, placed on the 1993 layout.
3. The Sicily DZ worldspace and the jump (aircraft, scene, power armor check). It's a complete
   moment on its own, and the most fun thing to show people.
4. The underground, joining the two.
5. Bragg Boulevard: the street, the market, the checkpoint, and the storm drain into the underground.
6. Factions, then quests.

## Building it

### Factions

- **New factions; never edit the Gunners faction.** Copy the Gunners' NPC templates, outfits and
  leveled lists into new records, for the soldiers and the settlers.
- Both start **neutral to the player**. A script on the quest stage where the player picks a side
  makes the other faction an enemy (like the Brotherhood and Institute split in vanilla).
- Restyle the soldiers toward early-90s battle dress: woodland camouflage, the old helmet,
  parachute badges.

### The map

- Area: the 1-504 barracks area as outlined, about 1.2 x 1.9 km, plus 150 m margin:
  **26 x 38 cells** (x -13 to 12, y -19 to 18), true scale.
- Terrain: USGS 3DEP 1 m lidar, 70 to 128 m elevation (57 m of relief).
  `scripts/make_heightmap.py` makes the 16-bit heightmap (`--exaggerate` for taller hills).
  Each worldspace's outline is in its `AREAS` list (`--area barracks`, the default, or
  `--area bragg_blvd` or `--area sicily`), and its files go to `out/<area>/`.
- The lidar is recent (2010s), so it shows today's roads and grading, not 1993's. Where they
  differ, the terrain follows the 1993 photo and gets smoothed by hand in the Creation Kit.
- Layout: USGS DOQ from **NAPP photo of 1993-02-23**, 1 m, in `data/doq_raw/`.
  `scripts/make_overlay.py` lines it up with the cell grid; `out/barracks/aerial_grid.png` shows
  each cell's Creation Kit coordinates.
  - Sicily: `O3507956.SWS` (Overhills SW, NAPP 6136-222, 1993-02-23) and `O3507955.SES` (Lobelia SE,
    NAPP 6139-132, 1993-02-24), in that order: `make_overlay.py --area sicily` with both.
  - Bragg Boulevard: the four Fayetteville quarter quads, `O3507857.NWS` (NAPP 6139-030,
    1993-02-24), `.NES` (6136-126, 1993-02-23), `.SWS` (6139-032, 1993-02-24) and `.SES`
    (6136-124, 1993-02-23), in that order: `make_overlay.py --area bragg_blvd` with all four.
- Importing: the Fallout 4 Creation Kit's heightmap editor (World menu) is now only an importer,
  and it reads a grayscale DDS of **one channel of 32-bit float** (DXGI R32_FLOAT); a normal DDS
  gives "The request is not supported". You give it the worldspace's cells and it says what image
  size it wants; you give it the lowest and highest height, and a wrong range squashes the terrain.
  `make_heightmap.py` writes `out/heightmap.dds` in that format (0 at the lowest point, 1 at the
  highest), one pixel per height point (833 x 1217), and prints the heights to enter (0 and 4020
  at true scale). If the Creation Kit asks for another size, rerun with `--dds-size WxH`.
- Fallback if the importer misbehaves: write the terrain records (LAND) straight into the plugin
  with an xEdit script. More work, but exact.
- **Tested 2026-10-23: it works.** The barracks heightmap went in through World → Heightmap
  Editing → **Heightmap Import** (Heightmap Autopaint is for texturing later) at cells x -13 to 12,
  y -19 to 18, min 0, max 4020, and walked in game (`cow DevilsBarracksWorld 0 0`): gently rolling
  ground, not stretched or flattened.
  Heights come through exactly: at the centre of cell 0, 0 (`cow` lands you at a cell's centre,
  not its corner) the game said z 2760, the heightmap 2765. So the import puts the lowest point at
  z 0, and anything built from the heightmap lines up with the ground.
- **The photo on the ground:** `scripts/make_photo_overlay.py` drapes the 1993 photo over the
  terrain as a reference mesh (one NIF, 16 units above the ground point for point, no collision),
  for finding your way and placing buildings. Placed once at 0, 0, 0 with **Is Full LOD** ticked
  (Persistent isn't offered for a static, and isn't needed): it shows from every corner of the
  map. Deleted when it's no longer needed. Needs PyNifly unpacked to `data/tools/`.
- Creation Kit setup: without a `CreationKitCustom.ini` in the game folder it doesn't read the
  texture archives and complains about missing standard textures. Don't unpack the archives; add
  `[Archive]` with `bInvalidateOlderFiles=1`, `sResourceDataDirsFinal=` and `SResourceArchiveList2=`
  listing `Fallout4 - Animations.ba2` and `Fallout4 - Textures1.ba2` to `Textures9.ba2` and
  `Fallout4 - TexturesPatch.ba2`.

### Weapons

The Devils should carry what Bragg carried, and the base game doesn't have it: no M16A2, no 1911,
no anti-materiel rifle, no M2. (.50 caliber ammo exists, but only the hunting rifle fires it, with
a modded receiver.)

- **Reuse vanilla animations; don't make new ones.** Most Fallout 4 weapon mods build a new model
  and fit it to an existing vanilla weapon's skeleton, so the vanilla animations drive it. The work
  in Blender is the model, the textures, and lining parts up with the vanilla bones (magazine to the
  magazine bone, bolt to the bolt bone). Modelling, not animation, and a good job for community
  help.

  | Weapon | Borrows animations from | Notes |
  |---|---|---|
  | **Anti-materiel rifle** (M82-style Barrett), .50 cal | Hunting rifle | The big one. Scarce: in the early 90s the Army had very few, mostly with special operations. The Devils keep a handful in the arms room, signed out like gold. |
  | **M16A2**, 5.56 | Combat rifle or assault rifle | The Devils' standard rifle. |
  | **1911**, .45 | 10mm pistol | Top's pearl-handled one is a reward (see "Rewards"). |
  | **M2 (Ma Deuce)**, .50 cal | None: a turret | A retextured vanilla machine-gun turret with .50 cal damage, on sandbagged positions like the Yadkin checkpoint. A carried or crewed version would need real animation work: later, if ever. |

- **Start cheap.** Until the models exist, retextured and renamed vanilla weapons stand in, so
  quests can be built and tested without waiting on art.
- **No hard dependency on Weaponsmith** (or any big gun pack). Requiring it would push a large,
  hard-to-deploy mod onto every player and tie this one to it. Instead:
  - **The mod works on its own**, with its own weapons.
  - **An optional patch** for players who already run Weaponsmith (or similar) swaps the Devils'
    leveled lists to that mod's Barrett and M16A2.
- **Bundling another author's standalone weapon** needs their permission, and their asset license
  may not fit this mod's CC BY-NC-SA art license. Making our own avoids both problems.

### Buildings

**The company barracks** (the 1950s "hammerhead" design; the first one to build is in cells
(-2, 9) and (-2, 8), and its twin stands just west of it):

- **Three floors**, flat roof, walls of precast concrete in a sandstone color. Outside, the walls
  are divided into bays by plain vertical pilasters, with a band at each floor line and two windows
  per bay on each floor (see `data/reference/barracks_exterior.jpg`, not in git).
- **The main wing** runs roughly north-south, leaning about 15° (the south end further east): about
  85 to 90 m long and 15 to 20 m wide, from the 1993 photo.
- **The one-story wing at the north end is an L:** the orderly room on the east, the **arms
  room** on the west as the short leg of the L, running further north than the rest. (The Devils' arms room in the story can be this room in
  every company's building.)
- **Four sets of main doors,** two on each long side, with short flights of steps and railings.
- **The L is one story.** The arms room sits on the main wing's north end, against the orderly
  room's west side, and runs further north than everything else, toward the road. The **orderly room** (circled in
  `data/reference/barracks_exterior_company_entrance.jpg`) stands east of the main wing's east
  wall, about 10 x 11 m, with the **company entrance** in its south face. Inside: the arms room
  door directly on the left, the **CQ desk** directly on the right, and behind (north of) the desk
  a hall running **east-west**: the **company commander's** office north of it, the **First
  Sergeant's** south of it (east of the CQ desk), and north of the hall at its west end two more
  rooms, a **bathroom** and a **supply closet**.
- **The arms room door is a Dutch door with a shelf:** top and bottom halves open separately, and
  the bottom half has a shelf across it for handing out weapons and NODs.
- **No way through inside.** The company entrance opens to the outside, just east of the main
  wing's east wall, and the orderly room and arms room don't connect to the main wing at all: you
  go outside one to get into the other. In the mod that makes them separate interior cells, each
  with its own door from outside.
- **Block-out:** `scripts/make_barracks_blockout.py` builds plain boxes at the measured size
  (main wing 85 x 17 x 11 m; the L's size is a guess, low buildings barely show in the photo),
  with collision, and prints where to place it: X -5339, Y 36039, Z 2202, rotation 349.1.
- **Main wing exterior:** `scripts/make_barracks.py` builds it from the photo's proportions:
  10 bays of about 8.5 m, paired strips at the bay lines, two windows per bay per floor (about
  1.9 x 1.2 m, a window's width apart), darker bands at the floor lines, plain end walls, a
  parapet cap, and **double doors** in bays 3 and 8 on both sides, each with a landing and steps
  running **straight out** from the wall to the ground, with a railing down each side. The main floor is 0.5 m above the highest
  ground under the wing.
- **The L's exterior:** the company entrance (double doors, one step up) at the west end of the
  orderly room's south face; windows for the First Sergeant (south and east), the CO (east and
  north) and a small high one for the bathroom; none for the supply closet. The **arms room has no
  windows and no outside door.** A band and a parapet cap match the main wing.
- Every hammerhead on the map is this one model, placed again and again.
- **Easter egg: room 305** (third floor, third door from the end of the hall) has a big boulder
  in the middle of the floor. It belonged to a soldier everyone called **Rock**: in basic, his
  drill sergeant made him carry a rock so he could tell his left from his right on the march, and
  the name stuck. Maybe a note on the rock, or a footlocker stencilled "ROCK". (Nickname only; no
  real names in the mod.)

- **Exterior:** a NIF shell (walls and roof, simple box collision), reused for every identical
  barracks. Split large buildings into a few pieces for occlusion and precombines. A simple LOD
  version for distance (xLODGen).
- **Interior:** a load door (teleport) to a separate interior cell, built from modular kit pieces.
  Room bounds and portals in the Creation Kit are only for occlusion inside big interiors; they don't
  load anything.
- Textures through `.bgsm` material files.
- **Collision needs the BSX "Havok" flag.** A mesh with collision but no `BSXFlags` on its root
  (or one without the Havok bit, 2) is walked straight through. Vanilla statics carry BSX 130
  (162 with an editor marker) and collision-object flags 128; the block-out script copies that.
- Tools: **PyNifly** for Blender (version 28.1.0 or later fixed Fallout 4 collision export; re-export
  anything made with earlier versions). Look for the old barracks NIF on the other drives.
